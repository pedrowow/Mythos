Nyx

Goddess of Night; one of the first emanations from Chaos

Primordial Night Darkness

Nyx is Night itself — not a goddess who governs the night, but the personification of darkness as a primordial condition. She emerges from Chaos as one of the first separations, paired with Erebus (deep Darkness), and together they produce Aether (the bright upper air) and Hemera (Day). Light is born from the union of two darknesses. It is a paradox the Greeks were comfortable with.

Nyx is remarkable for the breadth of her children, which Hesiod lists as an extended catalogue of abstractions: Sleep (Hypnos) and Death (Thanatos), Dreams, Strife, Deception, Old Age, Doom, Fate, Nemesis. She is the mother of most of the dark forces that operate on the margins of mythology — not evil, but ancient and unavoidable.

Even Zeus, according to some traditions, feared her. There is a story in which Zeus wants to punish Hypnos (Sleep, her son) for helping Hera, but holds back when Hypnos takes shelter with Nyx — unwilling to provoke her anger. The king of the gods deferring to the Night. It is one of the clearest indications of how the Greeks understood the hierarchy of primordials versus Olympians: the old forces precede and exceed the new ones, even when the new ones rule.
