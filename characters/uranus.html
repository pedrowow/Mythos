Uranus

The Sky; first ruler of the cosmos; father of the Titans

Primordial Sky First Ruler

Uranus — the Sky — is the first patriarch of Greek mythology, and the template for every bad father that follows. Born from Gaia alone, he became her husband and covered her entirely, a sky pressing down upon the earth. Their union produced the Titans, the Cyclopes, and the Hecatoncheires — and Uranus, fearing the power of his own children, buried them back inside Gaia rather than let them exist in the world.

His removal from power is abrupt and permanent. Cronus, armed by Gaia with a sickle, castrated him during the night. Uranus retreated — bleeding, cursing, already diminished — and never returned to prominence. He appears afterwards only as a voice, pronouncing the prophecy that Cronus too will fall. His legacy is entirely negative: a lesson in how fear of succession guarantees it.

The severed genitals, thrown into the sea, produced sea foam from which Aphrodite emerged — one of mythology's stranger causal chains. It is as if even in his mutilation, Uranus managed to generate something beautiful, though entirely by accident.
