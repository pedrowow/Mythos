Chaos

The primordial void; the first condition of existence

Primordial Void Origin

Chaos is not a character in any conventional sense. It does not act, speak, or pursue goals. It is the condition that precedes all conditions — the formless void before differentiation, the open gap from which everything else emerged. Hesiod gives us the word without fully explaining it, which is appropriate: an explanation would already be an imposition of order on something that preceded order.

The Greek word khaos means a yawning gap or chasm, not the tumultuous disorder the word suggests in modern English. The ancient conception was of emptiness and openness — a space in which things could appear — rather than of violent confusion. Our modern sense of "chaos" as disorder is a later semantic shift.

From Chaos came Nyx (Night), Erebus (Darkness), Gaia (Earth), and Tartarus (the Abyss). These are not so much children as aspects of Chaos becoming distinct enough to have separate names — the void differentiating into something more structured. The universe, in this account, is not created; it self-organises.
